# 05. Mention categorization rubric

How to fill in the spreadsheet columns consistently. Inconsistent categorization makes the data useless after 3 months.

## Mention type

Pick one. Most specific applies.

| Type | When to use |
|---|---|
| Featured in article | Brand is the subject or one of 2 to 3 subjects of the piece |
| Listed in article | Brand appears in a list/roundup of 4+ items |
| Passing reference | Brand mentioned in one sentence in a larger piece |
| Quoted | A statement from your brand or founder is quoted |
| Linked from article | Brand URL linked without prominent mention |
| Forum mention | Mention on Reddit, HN, Stack Exchange, niche forum |
| Social post | Mention on X, LinkedIn, Threads, Mastodon |
| Comment | Mention in a comment on someone else's content |
| Podcast mention | Mention during a podcast episode |
| Video mention | Mention in a video (YouTube, TikTok, Vimeo) |
| Review | A standalone review of your product/service |
| Comparison | Brand named in "X vs Y" or "X alternative" content |
| Mention in directory | Listed in a directory or aggregator |
| Press release pickup | Syndicated press release republished |
| Other | Anything that doesn't fit |

## Sentiment

Three categories, no half-measures. If a mention is mixed, pick the dominant tone.

- **Positive**: recommendation, praise, citation as authoritative, or "I use this and it works"
- **Neutral**: mention without judgment, listing among options, factual reference
- **Negative**: criticism, complaint, comparison where you lose, public dispute

Note: critical-but-fair reviews are still Negative even if you agree with them. Sentiment tracks how the audience perceives it, not whether you accept it.

## Linked back to us

- **Yes**: the source includes a clickable link to your domain
- **No**: brand mentioned by name only
- **Branded text only**: brand name appears as text but not hyperlinked

The Yes/No distinction matters because backlinks help SEO; brand-name-only mentions help GEO (AI engines).

## Action taken

Pick one. Update when status changes.

- **Logged**: recorded in tracker, no further action needed
- **Thank-you sent**: outreach sent to the author/source
- **Correction requested**: contacted them to fix a factual error
- **Link requested**: asked them to add a link (only if mention is positive)
- **Reply in thread**: replied in the public thread (forums, social)
- **Internal share**: shared with team/audience
- **Escalated**: handed off to legal, PR, or executive review (for negative mentions that warrant response)

If you fail to take action you intended to, leave the field as "Logged" and add a note about why.

## Author authority

This is judgment, not a formula. Use rough buckets:

- **High**: established outlet (TechCrunch, mainstream news), high-follower author in your niche (10k+ relevant followers), recognized expert
- **Medium**: niche blog with regular publishing, moderately-followed author (1k to 10k), domain authority
- **Low**: small blog, low-follower account, anonymous user, aggregator

Pre-decide a threshold for "I will respond to this" vs "I will only log it." Most indie brands cannot reach out to every mention; the High/Medium gate is fine.

## Context excerpt

Copy the actual sentence (or two) that mentions your brand. NOT a summary. The exact wording matters because:

- You will quote it in outreach
- You will spot pattern (everyone keeps describing you with the same phrase)
- You will catch errors (people calling you something you are not)

Cap at 300 characters. Use ellipsis if longer.

## What NOT to log

- Your own posts and your own social media
- Posts your team made (employee advocacy is fine but separate metric)
- Affiliate links from paid affiliates (track those separately as affiliate revenue, not organic mentions)
- Bot/spam mentions
- Mentions on aggregators that scrape your own content without adding anything

These dilute the signal. Either log them in a separate sheet or skip entirely.
