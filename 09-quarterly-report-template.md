# 09. Quarterly mention report

Every three months. 60 to 90 minutes. Build the report for yourself (if solo) or for stakeholders. Format below; adapt section headers to your audience.

## Report structure

### 1. Headline numbers (one page)

```
Q[N] 20[YY] mention summary

Total mentions: [N]
Change vs prior quarter: [+X% / -X% / flat]

Breakdown:
- Positive: [N] ([%])
- Neutral: [N] ([%])
- Negative: [N] ([%])

Unique source domains: [N]
New sources this quarter: [N]
Repeat sources (mentioned us 2+ times): [N]

Mentions with backlinks: [N] ([%])
Mentions with quoted attribution: [N] ([%])

AI engine direct test:
- Mentioned in ChatGPT: [N of 5 prompts]
- Mentioned in Claude: [N of 5 prompts]
- Mentioned in Perplexity: [N of 5 prompts]
- Cited by URL in any engine: [Yes/No, which URLs]
```

### 2. Top 5 mentions of the quarter

For each, one paragraph:
- Source and author
- What they said (one sentence)
- Why this one matters
- Action taken

These are the mentions you would point to if asked "how is GEO going?"

### 3. Top 5 themes in mentions

Look across all mentions for repeated patterns:

- Are people describing your brand the same way? (good)
- Are they describing you the wrong way? (problem)
- Are they comparing you to specific competitors over and over? (interesting)
- Are they consistently citing one page of your site? (signal that page is strongest)
- Are they consistently complaining about the same thing? (signal to fix)

One paragraph per theme.

### 4. AI engine test results

Tabular view. Same 5 prompts, 4 engines, this quarter vs last quarter:

| Prompt | ChatGPT | Claude | Perplexity | Google AIO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. What is [brand] | Cited (last: Mentioned) | Mentioned (last: Not mentioned) | Cited (last: Cited) | Mentioned (last: Mentioned) |
| 2. Best [category] | Mentioned (last: Mentioned) | Not mentioned (last: Not mentioned) | Mentioned (last: Not mentioned) | Not mentioned (last: Not mentioned) |
| ... |

Annotate with the direction (improving, flat, declining) for each.

### 5. Sources you wish would mention you but did not

The aspirational list. Specific outlets, podcasts, newsletters, communities where mention would be a milestone.

For each, note:
- Did anything you did this quarter increase the likelihood of mention?
- Is there a realistic path?
- Is this the right outlet, or is it ego-driven?

Sometimes the right answer is "I have been wanting Outlet X to mention us for a year and it is not going to happen; let it go."

### 6. Actions for next quarter

Cap at 5. Each action is specific, time-bound, and has a single owner (even if owner = you).

Example actions:
- "Write guest post for [specific outlet] by [date]; first draft to [editor] by [earlier date]"
- "Pitch [specific podcast] in [specific week] with [specific angle]"
- "Fix the description on the homepage that AI engines keep misquoting; ship by [date]"
- "Reach out to [specific journalist] who has covered adjacent brands; intro email by [date]"

Actions without dates do not happen. Actions without specific recipients do not happen.

### 7. Lessons learned

Three to five sentences. What did you try this quarter that worked? What did not? What surprised you?

This section is for your future self. Reread it at the start of the next quarter.

## How to deliver

### If solo / for yourself

Save as a markdown file. Filename: `qN-YYYY-mention-report.md`. Keep them all in one folder. The history compounds.

### If for stakeholders (investors, advisors, team)

Convert the markdown to a Google Doc or PDF. Add a one-page TLDR at the top: headline numbers and the top 3 actions.

Stakeholders read the TLDR. The full report is reference material for anyone who wants depth.

### If for the public (rare)

Some indie brands publish their quarterly numbers as build-in-public content. If you do:

- Numbers only. Skip the strategy section (competitors read it).
- Skip the aspirational outlets list (they will see it).
- Frame as learning, not boasting.
- Be the same person whether numbers were up or down.

## What makes a quarterly report worth the time

It is worth the time if you act on it. If you write it, file it, and never reference it again, do not write it.

The discipline: at the start of each quarter, open the previous quarter's report and re-read the "Actions for next quarter" section. For each action:

- Did you do it?
- If yes, did it work?
- If no, why not?

This loop closes the cycle. Without the loop, the report is theatre.

## When to stop running this

If after 4 quarters of reports you cannot point to a single concrete change you made based on the data, stop running quarterly reports. Go back to monthly reviews only.

The reports are an investment that should pay back; if they don't, the time is better spent on the content itself.
