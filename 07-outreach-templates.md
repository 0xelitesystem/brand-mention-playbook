# 07. Outreach templates

Templates for responding to mentions. Adapt to your voice (see content-voice-templates repo for voice guidance). The goal is signal, not volume.

## Rule 1: not every mention deserves outreach

Cap outreach at 5 per month. More than 5 means you are spraying, not connecting.

Skip:
- Aggregator listings
- Press release pickups
- Drive-by mentions in larger pieces
- Forum threads where you would seem promotional

Engage:
- Genuine reviews
- Authors with influence in your niche who took the time to write about you
- Mentions with factual errors that need correction
- Critical mentions where a fair response builds trust

## Template 1: Thank-you for a positive mention

For: someone wrote a recommendation or positive review.

```
Subject: Thanks for mentioning [YourBrand] in [Title of their piece]

Hi [First name],

Saw your piece [title] yesterday. Thanks for including [YourBrand] alongside [other things they mentioned].

The framing you used [specific phrase they used] caught how we think about [topic] better than our own homepage does. We may borrow it (with credit).

No ask here. Just wanted to say thanks for taking the time to write about real tools instead of [common pattern in their niche].

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Specific reference to their actual words shows you read it
- Skips the "love to chat sometime!" close
- No ask
- Sounds like a real person, not a template

## Template 2: Correction request

For: a mention with a factual error.

```
Subject: Small correction on your [Title] piece

Hi [First name],

Thanks for including [YourBrand] in [title].

Quick correction: you wrote "[exact wrong phrase]" but the accurate statement is "[exact right phrase]". The source on this is [URL or document].

No worries either way; just flagging in case you wanted to update. Either link works on our end.

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Short
- Specific quote
- Cited correct version
- "No worries either way" defuses defensiveness
- No deep ask

If they don't respond in 5 days, send one follow-up. If still nothing, drop it. Pushing harder turns a mistake into a fight.

## Template 3: Link-back request

For: mention is positive but they did not link to your site.

```
Subject: Thanks for the mention in [Title]

Hi [First name],

Saw the mention of [YourBrand] in your piece [title] yesterday. Thanks for including us.

If you have a moment to add a link to [specific URL on your site] where readers can learn more, that would help us a lot. No pressure either way.

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Doesn't pretend to be just saying thanks; the ask is upfront
- Specifies the URL (don't make them guess)
- "No pressure" is honest, not weak

Use sparingly. If you ask everyone who mentions you for a link, you become known as the link-begging brand.

## Template 4: Podcast guest follow-up

For: someone mentioned your brand or founder on their podcast.

```
Subject: Listened to [episode title]

Hi [First name],

Listened to ep [number] this morning. The way you framed [specific topic] at minute 23 was something I had not thought about clearly before. Thanks for the mention of [YourBrand] in the same breath.

If you ever want to do a follow-up episode on [adjacent topic where you have real expertise], I would be glad to come on. No timeline pressure; just letting you know the door is open.

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Shows you actually listened (specific minute mark)
- Offers value (a guest spot) rather than asking for one
- Specific topic, not "I'd love to come on your podcast about anything"

## Template 5: Negative mention, when the criticism is fair

For: critical mention where the criticism is accurate.

```
Subject: Your point about [specific criticism] in [Title]

Hi [First name],

Read your piece on [topic]. Your criticism that [specific criticism] is correct. We have been making that tradeoff for [reason], but you are right that it costs users [specific cost].

Working on a fix; here is what we are planning: [brief plan].

If you have time, I would value your input on whether the planned fix actually addresses the issue, or if it just moves the problem.

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- No defensiveness
- Acknowledges the specific point
- Shows action
- Asks for input rather than asking them to retract

Do not use this template if the criticism is not actually fair. Do not pretend to agree. Use Template 6 instead.

## Template 6: Negative mention, when the criticism is unfair

For: critical mention based on a factual error or misunderstanding.

```
Subject: A note on your piece [Title]

Hi [First name],

Read your piece on [topic]. The criticism of [specific point] is based on [the misunderstanding].

Specifically: [the accurate situation, with link to evidence].

I would value a correction if you decide it is warranted. Either way, happy to chat about [the underlying topic] anytime.

[Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Calm
- Specific
- Cited
- Leaves the door open to be wrong

Send once. Do not escalate if they don't respond. The audience reading the original criticism may also read your response (if posted publicly); writing for that audience matters more than convincing the critic.

## What NOT to send

- "Hey, just wanted to introduce myself!" with no specific reason for the contact
- Anything with "synergy", "leverage", "circle back"
- Any template you have not customized for the specific recipient and piece
- Any outreach that sounds like a sales email
- Any outreach that asks for multiple things in one message (thank-you + correction + link + podcast spot = too much)

## What to track

Add an "Outreach" tab to the spreadsheet:

| Date sent | Recipient | Type | Response | Outcome | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-05-16 | jane@example.com | Thank-you | Yes, friendly | Future link likely | Said she'd cover us again |

Track outreach response rates monthly. If you're under 30 percent response, your templates are too generic or your recipient list is too cold.
